Hg-Ne Lamp

The Hg-Ne Lamp provides the light that will eject electrons from the metal. Mercury is an ideal light source for this purpose because it emits a lot of light, but only in a small number of wavelengths.

Potentiometer

The voltage between the photocathode and the anode is adjusted by turning the knob of a variable resistor (also known as a potentiometer).

Use Tool

Keithley Model 6514 Electrometer

The electrometer measures the photocurrent (i.e., the current that flows from the cathode to the anode when light hits the cathode). It is an especially sophisticated instrument that can reliably detect fractions of a picoamp. (1 pA = 10-12 A)

Keithley Model 2000 Digital Multimeter (DMM)

ThorLabs® Absorptive Neutral Density Filters

A neutral density (ND) filter attenuates light uniformly across a wide range of wavelengths. This filter wheel places one (or none) of five different ND filters in front of the Hg-discharge lamp, changing the intensity of the light (i.e., the number of photons per unit area) that falls on the photocathode.

Use Tool

Click on a filter to rotate it into position.

Edumund Optics® Bandpass Interference Filters

This device places one of four different bandpass filters in front of the Hg-discharge lamp, allowing you to limit the light that hits the photocathode to a narrow range of the Hg-Ne lamp's spectrum.

Use Tool

Click on a filter to rotate it into position.
